# First Time in Cape Town: 1 Week
![Stunning landscape of Cape Town's iconic Table Mountain under a bright blue sky.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/36597753/pexels-photo-36597753.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)
*Photo by [Adrien Olichon](https://www.pexels.com/@adrien-olichon-1257089)*

Cape Town sits on a narrow peninsula wedged between the Atlantic Ocean and Table Mountain. For a first-time visitor, the city offers an intense mix of dramatic coastal scenery, world-class dining, and complex history. It is a place where urban life dissolves into wilderness within a twenty-minute drive.

Planning an initial trip requires balancing famous landmarks with the rhythm of local neighborhoods. Rather than trying to cover the entire Western Cape, stick to the city bowl, peninsula coastline, and nearby winelands. Keep your schedule flexible, as weather patterns ultimately dictate outdoor activities here.

## One Week (7 Days) in Cape Town

### Days 1–2
Focus your first two days on the Atlantic Seaboard and City Bowl. Start early at the Victoria & Alfred Waterfront for orientation, then take the aerial cableway up Table Mountain around midday when clouds clear. In the late afternoon, follow the coastal route from Sea Point Promenade down to Camps Bay for sunset drinks along the strip.
*Timing:* Begin at 8:00 AM each day to avoid peak crowds; reserve cableway trips for clear afternoons.
*Practical Warning:* High winds regularly shut down the Table Mountain Cableway without notice. Check the official live updates online before traveling to the base station, and move Table Mountain to day one if the sky is clear.

### Days 3–4
Dedicate these days to the Cape Peninsula loop. Drive south along the False Bay coastline, stopping in the harbor village of Kalk Bay and Boulders Beach in Simon's Town. Proceed south to the Cape Point Nature Reserve, then loop back north via the toll road along Chapman's Peak Drive toward Hout Bay.
*Timing:* Depart by 7:30 AM to beat tour buses; plan for a full 8- to 9-hour excursion.
*Practical Warning:* Chacma baboons along the Cape Point route and coastal parking lots are habituated to humans. Keep vehicle windows rolled up and do not carry visible food bags, as they will aggressively open unlocked car doors.

### Days 5–6
Shift inland toward Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden before heading into the Constantia Valley or the neighboring Cape Winelands of Stellenbosch and Franschhoek. Spend these days moving between historic wine estates, tasting rooms, and shaded garden lunch spots.
*Timing:* Operating hours for most wine estates run from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM.
*Practical Warning:* South Africa enforces a near-zero legal blood-alcohol limit for driving. If you plan to visit multiple wineries, hire a designated driver or use a specialized tour service instead of self-driving.

### Day 7
Spend your final day exploring urban culture and heritage. Walk through the brightly painted streets of the Bo-Kaap neighborhood, visit the District Six Museum, and browse local artisan shops along Kloof Street or the East City precinct.
*Timing:* Start around 9:00 AM and finish by mid-afternoon.
*Practical Warning:* Central Business District streets empty out rapidly after business hours. Avoid walking alone through downtown commercial streets like Strand or Adderley after 6:00 PM.

## Getting Around
The standard and safest method for visitors moving around Cape Town is Uber, which operates with high driver availability across the city bowl and Atlantic seaboard. For a cheaper public alternative along major coastal corridors, purchase a MyConnect card at MyCiTi bus stations; this system connects the airport, downtown, and Sea Point efficiently. Self-driving is recommended only for day trips along the Cape Peninsula and Winelands.

## Local Tip
First-time visitors are often confused by "car guards"—individuals wearing high-visibility vests who direct you into street parking spots and watch your vehicle while you are away. Most are self-employed locals working for tips rather than official municipal employees. When returning to your car, a standard tip of R5 to R10 (in cash) is expected for short stays, or R20 for overnight parking. You do not need to pay upfront; pay only when you return to an undamaged car.
