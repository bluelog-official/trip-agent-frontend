# Hapjeong Filming Street: A K-Pop Walk You Can Enter

Hapjeong and the nearby Sangsu streets show up in dramas and music videos because they are ordinary slopes with murals, practice rooms, and small shops. That is also why the walk works. You do not need a fan-club lottery. You need comfortable shoes, a short list of corners that are public, and the manners of someone walking through a neighborhood where people live and rehearse.

## A morning loop, not a stakeout

### What you can actually see

Start at Hapjeong Station and walk toward the streets used as filming backdrops rather than toward a company lobby with security. Public murals, record shops, and cafe windows are the visit. A lobby where trainees enter is not a viewpoint. Do not wait at a door, do not follow someone in practice clothes, and do not block a scooter lane for a photo. If a corner is taped or a shop says no filming, that instruction is the whole rule.

The walk itself is one to two hours if you include a coffee. Stretching it into a hunt for a celebrity is how you waste the day and annoy the block. Treat a surprise sighting, if it happens, the way you would treat anyone on a sidewalk: do not touch, do not run, leave the frame.

### Shops that belong on the route

A small record or poster shop is the souvenir that fits a bag. Budget ₩10,000 to ₩30,000 if you buy one pressing or a print, and nothing if you are only looking. Cafe coffee is ₩4,500 to ₩7,000. Eat a real lunch back toward Hongdae or Mangwon, where a noodle or a rice plate is ₩10,000 to ₩14,000, instead of calling a pastry the meal.

## Pair it with one ticketed thing or with none

This street is the free half of a K-pop day. The other half can be a Hongdae live room at night or nothing. Do not add a Jamsil concert and a company exhibit on the same date. Hapjeong is valuable because it is slow. Rushing it to make a dome soundcheck removes the only pleasure, which is being on foot in a real neighborhood.

If it rains, the slopes are slick and the murals are not improved by a crowd under two umbrellas. Switch the plan to an indoor exhibit you already hold a ticket for, or to a record shop you can stand inside. Forcing the photo walk in a downpour is how phones and knees get hurt.

## Local tip

Download the map before you leave the station. Alleys in this pocket do not announce themselves in English, and a fan blog's nickname for a corner will not match the sign on the utility pole. Follow the station exit number you saved, then the Korean street name, then stop when the loop returns you to a main road.
