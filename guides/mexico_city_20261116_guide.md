# Where to Eat and Wander in Mexico City
![A stunning aerial view of Chapultepec Park with cityscape and mountains in the background.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/28577135/pexels-photo-28577135.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Sitting 2,240 meters above sea level, Mexico City (CDMX) is an immense, vibrant metropolis defined by distinct neighborhood pockets rather than a single unified center. For a first-time visitor, navigating its tree-lined avenues, ancient Aztec foundations, and world-class culinary landscape can feel overwhelming without a clear strategy.

The key to enjoying the city is pacing yourself. Group your activities by geographical zones to avoid spending hours trapped in infamous gridlock, drink plenty of water to adjust to the altitude, and embrace the local rhythm of long afternoon lunches followed by late-night taco runs.

## 5 Days in Mexico City

### Day 1: Historic Center and Aztec Roots

* Neighborhood Route: Begin at the sprawling Zócalo central plaza, inspect the excavated ruins of the Templo Mayor, and marvel at the interior of the Metropolitan Cathedral. Walk west down the pedestrian-only Avenida Francisco I. Madero toward the tiled façade of Casa de los Azulejos, finishing your afternoon at the ornate Palacio de Bellas Artes.
* Timing: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM
* Practical Warning: Keep a close eye on personal belongings along Avenida Madero; pickpocketing is common in dense pedestrian crowds, especially near metro entrances.

### Day 2: Art and Canals in Coyoacán and Xochimilco

* Neighborhood Route: Travel south early to Xochimilco for a traditional wooden boat ride along the ancient ecological canals. By midday, head north to the cobblestone streets of Coyoacán to tour Frida Kahlo’s Casa Azul, browse Mercado de Coyoacán, and enjoy coffee in Plaza Hidalgo.
* Timing: 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM
* Practical Warning: Tickets for Casa Azul are strictly sold online and routinely sell out weeks in advance; you will be turned away if you show up without a pre-booked reservation.

### Day 3: Architecture and Greenery in Roma and Condesa

* Neighborhood Route: Start with an espresso near Parque México, taking in the shaded Art Deco residential buildings of Condesa. Cross Avenida Insurgentes into Roma Norte to explore modern galleries along Calle Colima, stop for a late lunch, and end your evening sipping mezcal along Avenida Álvaro Obregón.
* Timing: 10:00 AM – 9:00 PM
* Practical Warning: Uneven volcanic-stone pavements and jutting tree roots create constant trip hazards; swap thin sandals or heels for sturdy walking shoes.

### Day 4: Museums and Elegance in Polanco and Chapultepec

* Neighborhood Route: Spend your morning inside Bosque de Chapultepec exploring the National Museum of Anthropology, one of the world's premier archaeological archives. In the afternoon, walk into the manicured enclave of Polanco to view the metallic architecture of Museo Soumaya and stroll down luxury corridor Avenida Presidente Masaryk.
* Timing: 9:30 AM – 6:30 PM
* Practical Warning: Most major state-run museums across Mexico City, including the Anthropology Museum, are strictly closed on Mondays. Plan your itinerary accordingly.

### Day 5: Local Markets and Modern Art in Juárez and San Rafael

* Neighborhood Route: Start in the revitalized neighborhood of Juárez along Calle Havre for independent design boutiques and third-wave coffee shops. Walk north across Paseo de la Reforma into quiet San Rafael to view contemporary installations at Museo Experimental El Eco, concluding with street food around Mercado San Cosme.
* Timing: 10:00 AM – 7:00 PM
* Practical Warning: San Rafael borders busy commercial corridors that clear out quickly after business hours; stick to main illuminated streets when walking after nightfall.

## Getting Around

The most efficient way to navigate Mexico City is to buy a Tarjeta de Movilidad Integrada (Integrated Mobility Card) at any Metro or Metrobús station for 15 MXN, then top it up with cash. This single rechargeable card unlocks the subterranean Metro system, the elevated Metrobús dedicated lanes that cut straight down major arteries like Avenida Insurgentes, and the Cablebús aerial transit lines. For late-night trips, long cross-town journeys, or traveling between distant neighborhoods after dark, rideshare apps like Uber and DiDi are widespread, affordable, and safer than flagging unmetered street taxis.

## Local Tip

When evaluating street food safety, skip the generic advice about avoiding raw salsa and focus instead on how a food stand manages cash. The safest and highest-turnover taco stalls assign a dedicated cashier who handles money while separate cooks handle the food with clean hands or gloves. If you see the person slicing your meat directly touching coins and paper bills with their bare hands, keep walking—no matter how long the line is.
