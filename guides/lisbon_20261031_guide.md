# See Lisbon in 2 Days (Weekend)
![Beautiful sunset over Lisbon with a view of the iconic 25 de Abril Bridge, framed by trees.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/33659330/pexels-photo-33659330.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Lisbon is defined by light, elevation, and centuries of maritime heritage. Set along the north bank of the Tagus River estuary, Portugal's capital balances old-world Mediterranean charm with a pragmatic modern edge. First-time visitors will quickly notice the city's terrain, defined by steep hills paved with slick calçada portuguesa mosaic cobblestones that reward every climb with panoramic lookout points called miradouros.

Two days are sufficient to capture Lisbon's core identity if you group sights geographically and pace your walking. Focus on the historic core—the winding alleys of Alfama and the elegant grid of Baixa—on your first day, before venturing west to the monument-heavy riverfront district of Belém on your second. Respect the geography, wear flat shoes with good grip, and treat the city's incline as an essential part of the experience.

## A Weekend in Lisbon

### Day 1 (Saturday)
Start at 8:30 AM in the historic Alfama neighborhood before the heat and tour groups arrive. Trace a route beginning at Miradouro de Santa Luzia, climbing past Lisbon Cathedral toward the Castelo de São Jorge. Spend the late morning walking down through the Graça district for expansive rooftop views, ending around 12:30 PM near the Santa Apolónia area. After a simple lunch of grilled fish or a pork bifana sandwich, head into the flat Baixa district by 2:30 PM. Walk up the grand Avenida da Liberdade, cross through Praça do Comércio, and finish your afternoon in Chiado and Bairro Alto as twilight approaches around 7:00 PM.

*Practical warning:* Do not board the iconic Tram 28 at main stops like Largo do Martim Moniz after 9:30 AM unless you want to wait up to an hour in line alongside pickpockets who target crowded cars.

### Day 2 (Sunday)
Begin early at 8:00 AM by taking the train or tram west to Belém, Lisbon's coastal gateway to the Age of Discovery. Start at the Jerónimos Monastery to beat the mid-morning entry queue, then walk across the gardens to the Monument to the Discoveries by 10:30 AM. Continue along the riverbank path toward the 16th-century Belém Tower. By 1:00 PM, return toward the central city, stopping at the Alcântara neighborhood to explore LX Factory, a refurbished industrial complex packed with design shops and eateries. Spend your late afternoon between 3:30 PM and 6:30 PM exploring the contemporary art collections at the MAAT museum or strolling along the Ribeira das Naus promenade.

*Practical warning:* Museums in Belém experience extreme ticket lines on Sundays. Purchase an electronic entry ticket online at least 24 hours in advance to bypass the primary ticket booth line completely.

## Getting Around
Lisbon's transit network includes metro lines, buses, historic trams, funiculars, and suburban trains. The simplest and most economical payment method for a short stay is the Navegante card, which can be purchased for €0.50 at any metro station ticket machine. Load it using the "Zapping" pay-as-you-go method by adding funds in increments of €5 or €10. Zapping lowers the cost of individual metro and tram journeys significantly compared to paying onboard with cash. For short hops up steep hills when public transit is inconvenient, localized rideshare services like Bolt and Uber operate continuously with typical inner-city fares running between €4 and €8.

## Local Tip
Skip the long queues and expensive tickets for the Santa Justa Lift (Elevador de Santa Justa). Designed by an apprentice of Gustave Eiffel, the iron elevator is visually impressive from street level, but waiting in line for an hour just to ride up two levels is a poor use of time. Instead, walk around to the back of the structure via the Carmo Convent ruins in Chiado. You can enter the top observation platform directly through the terrace near the Carmo Archaeological Museum for free, enjoying the exact same elevated panorama over Baixa without paying a fee or standing in line.
