# Cape Town Day by Day: 1 Week
![Stunning landscape of Cape Town's iconic Table Mountain under a bright blue sky.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/36597753/pexels-photo-36597753.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)
*Photo by [Adrien Olichon](https://www.pexels.com/@adrien-olichon-1257089)*

Cape Town sits at the intersection of dramatic mountain peaks and two crashing oceans, offering a landscape density rarely matched by other coastal capitals. For first-time visitors, navigating the city requires understanding its microclimates and steep topography, which can shift from blazing sunshine to dense coastal fog in a matter of minutes.

A well-planned week balances iconic high-altitude views with coastal drives, deep historical context, and the region's celebrated culinary scene. By organizing your itinerary around geographic hubs, you will minimize transit time across the Peninsula and stay ahead of the unpredictable Atlantic winds.

## One Week (7 Days) in Cape Town

### Days 1–2

Explore the historic core, the City Bowl, Bo-Kaap, Table Mountain, and the Victoria & Alfred (V&A) Waterfront. 

On Day 1, begin early at 08:00 at the Table Mountain Aerial Cableway station to beat both the afternoon wind and long queues. Allocate two hours at the summit before descending for a walk through the brightly painted streets of Bo-Kaap between 11:30 and 13:30. Spend the late afternoon strolling through the historic Company's Garden and end your evening at the V&A Waterfront. On Day 2, reserve 09:00 to 13:00 for the Robben Island ferry and tour, leaving the late afternoon for the Zeitz Museum of Contemporary Art Africa (MOCAA).

Practical warning: Table Mountain cable car operations are suspended immediately when wind speeds exceed safety thresholds. Check the operator's live website status at 07:00 and buy open-dated tickets online in advance to skip the ticket office queue.

### Days 3–4

Shift focus to the Atlantic Seaboard and southern slopes, covering Camps Bay, Clifton, Sea Point, and Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden.

Dedicate the morning of Day 3 (08:00–11:00) to hiking up Lion’s Head or walking the paved Sea Point Promenade. Spend the warm afternoon hours between 12:00 and 16:00 relaxing on Clifton 4th Beach. On Day 4, head inland to Kirstenbosch Botanical Garden from 09:00 to 13:00 to explore the canopy walkway and fynbos paths. In the late afternoon, drive along the cliffside pass of Chapman’s Peak Drive toward Hout Bay to catch the sunset around 18:00.

Practical warning: Ocean temperatures along the Atlantic Seaboard remain cold year-round, averaging 10°C to 13°C (50°F to 55°F). Strong rip currents are common at Clifton and Camps Bay, so only swim where lifesavers are actively stationed.

### Days 5–6

Execute a full Peninsula loop through Muizenberg, Kalk Bay, Simon's Town, and the Cape of Good Hope Nature Reserve.

Depart the City Bowl by 07:30 on Day 5 to stay ahead of coastal traffic. Reach the fishing harbor of Kalk Bay by 08:30 for breakfast, then proceed to the African penguin colony at Boulders Beach in Simon's Town between 10:30 and 12:30. Spend 13:30 to 17:00 exploring Cape Point and the Cape of Good Hope before returning via the quiet village of Scarborough. Reserve Day 6 for a slower morning browsing Kalk Bay's antique shops and enjoying fresh seafood along the harbor wall.

Practical warning: The Chacma baboons in the Cape Point reserve and near Simon's Town are habituated to humans and can be aggressive. Keep your vehicle windows completely rolled up in parking areas and never carry open food bags in their presence.

### Day 7

Conclude with a culture and wine day split between the Woodstock urban art district and the historical Constantia Valley.

Start Day 7 at 09:00 in Woodstock, taking a guided street-art walking tour or visiting the local design studios at the Old Biscuit Mill until 12:00. Transition to the Constantia Wine Route—the oldest wine-producing region in the Southern Hemisphere—for lunch and tastings from 13:00 to 17:00 at historic estates such as Groot Constantia or Klein Constantia.

Practical warning: South Africa enforces a near-zero legal blood-alcohol limit for drivers (0.02% BAC). If you intend to sample wines, hire a private driver for the afternoon or rely entirely on ride-hailing services.

## Getting Around

The simplest and safest way to move around central Cape Town and its inner suburbs is via ride-hailing apps like Uber, which operate reliably day and night. For public transit within the City Bowl, Sea Point, and along the main trunk route to the airport, use the MyCiTi bus system. You must purchase a rechargeable MyConnect card at main stations (such as the Civic Centre depot) before boarding, as drivers do not accept cash. Avoid unmetered street taxis and walking alone after dark outside heavily lit commercial strips.

## Local Tip

Download a local load-shedding tracking application like EskomSePush the moment you arrive. South Africa periodically manages power grid demand through scheduled rolling blackouts known locally as load-shedding. These outages cause traffic lights to shut off and disrupt cell phone towers for two- to four-hour blocks. While large hotels and major shopping malls run on backup diesel generators, smaller independent cafes, boutique shops, and card-payment terminals in neighborhoods like Kalk Bay or Woodstock often drop offline completely during scheduled slots. Always keep a small reserve of cash in South African Rand and carry a portable power bank in your daypack to keep your phone charged for navigation.
