# 5 Days in Marrakech: A Slower Route
![View of historic red walls and lush gardens under a blue sky in Marrakesh, Morocco.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/15360686/pexels-photo-15360686.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)
*Photo by [Moussa Idrissi](https://www.pexels.com/@mographe)*

Marrakech is infamous for its relentless sensory pulse. Between the shouting stallholders, dodging mopeds in waist-wide alleys, and the aromatic clouds of roasted cumin, first-time visitors often try to sprint through the sights. Doing so usually leads to burnout by day two. Stepping back and allowing the city to unfold over five days transforms a chaotic trip into a rich, manageable experience.

This slower itinerary balances the heavy hitters of the ancient Medina with the breezy, mid-century avenues of Gueliz. By staggering your sightseeing around the heat of the day and exploring distinct neighborhood loops, you will discover quiet courtyard gardens, artisan workshops, and low-key residential pockets that tour groups routinely miss.

## 5 Days in Marrakech

### Day 1
* Neighborhood Route: Central Medina, moving from Jemaa el-Fnaa through the Koutoubia Gardens into the Mouassine quarter.
* Timing: Morning from 8:30 AM to 12:30 PM for quiet walks through Mouassine; late afternoon from 4:30 PM to 7:30 PM to watch Jemaa el-Fnaa transition from open square to night market.
* Practical Warning: Ignore individuals near street corners who claim a street or monument is "closed today." This is a classic scam designed to redirect you into a high-pressure tannery tour or carpet store.

### Day 2
* Neighborhood Route: Southern Medina, covering the Kasbah and Mellah districts, with visits to Bahia Palace and El Badi Palace.
* Timing: Morning from 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM for Bahia Palace before tour buses arrive; afternoon from 3:00 PM to 6:00 PM exploring the spice markets of the historic Jewish Quarter.
* Practical Warning: Ticket counters at historical sites like Bahia Palace only accept cash in exact Moroccan Dirham. Credit card terminals are rarely available or frequently offline.

### Day 3
* Neighborhood Route: Northern Medina, focusing on Ben Youssef Madrasa, Souk Cherifia, and Le Jardin Secret.
* Timing: Early morning from 8:30 AM to 11:30 AM for Ben Youssef Madrasa; mid-afternoon from 2:30 PM to 5:30 PM unwinding inside Le Jardin Secret.
* Practical Warning: Alleyways in the northern souks are narrow and shared with delivery scooters. Always walk along the right wall and step into a doorway if you hear an approaching motor; never step backward suddenly without checking behind you.

### Day 4
* Neighborhood Route: Ville Nouvelle, shifting to Gueliz and Majorelle to visit Jardin Majorelle, the Yves Saint Laurent Museum, and Rue de la Liberté.
* Timing: Morning from 8:00 AM to 12:00 PM at Jardin Majorelle and the YSL Museum; afternoon from 2:30 PM to 6:30 PM browsing modern art galleries and design boutiques in Gueliz.
* Practical Warning: Jardin Majorelle no longer sells tickets at the gate. You must purchase timed-entry tickets online on their official website at least 48 to 72 hours in advance.

### Day 5
* Neighborhood Route: Western Fringe and Kennaria, exploring Menara Gardens and Hivernage parks, ending with a final rooftop sunset in Kennaria.
* Timing: Morning from 10:00 AM to 1:00 PM in the shady Menara olive groves; evening from 5:00 PM to 8:00 PM enjoying mint tea on a Kennaria rooftop.
* Practical Warning: Taxis picking up passengers near luxury hotels in Hivernage routinely refuse to switch on the taximeter. Insist firmly on the meter ("compteur") or walk 50 meters away from the hotel entrance to flag down a passing cab on the street.

## Getting Around
 navigation within the Medina is strictly on foot due to the density of the alleyways. For trips crossing between the Medina and the modern district of Gueliz, the simplest option is the small beige "Petit Taxi." These cabs carry up to three passengers and operate on a taximeter by law. Always check that the driver resets the meter upon entry; a standard ride across town during the day should cost between 15 and 30 MAD. If you prefer public transit, ALSA operates reliable city buses, notably Bus #19, which runs a direct circuit connecting Marrakesh Menara Airport to Jemaa el-Fnaa and the central train station for 30 MAD round-trip.

## Local Tip
GPS signals degrade quickly inside the high, narrow brick walls of the Medina, causing phone maps to jump erratically and leave you stranded in dead-end alleys. Before setting off each day, download an offline map layer (such as Organic Maps or Google Offline Maps) specifically for central Marrakech. More importantly, ignore your screen when navigating complex souks and look up at the building corners: major routes carry small, painted hexagonal metal signs pointing toward main exit points like "Jemaa el-Fnaa" or "Bab Doukkala." Following these physical wall markers will keep you moving in the right direction far better than satellite navigation ever can.
