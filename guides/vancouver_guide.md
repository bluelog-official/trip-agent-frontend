# Field Notes from Vancouver: 1 Week
![Stunning view of Vancouver cityscape with colorful sunset sky reflecting on harbor water.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/18604163/pexels-photo-18604163.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)
*Photo by [Adi K](https://www.pexels.com/@adi-k-182036)*

Vancouver occupies a dramatic geographic pocket between the Strait of Georgia and the steep, rain-softened slopes of the Coast Mountains. It is a city defined by dense urban living tucked directly against temperate rainforest, where high-rise residential towers glass off the salt air of Pacific inlets. For first-time visitors, the appeal lies in this immediate proximity to nature, alongside an exceptional East Asian food landscape shaped by decades of immigration.

Navigating the city well requires calibrating your expectations to its rhythms. Vancouver is casual, early-to-rise, and heavily dependent on microclimates created by the surrounding topography. Rain is a constant factor outside the mid-summer months, but the city rarely pauses for weather. A successful week here balances urban neighborhood exploration on foot with tactical excursions into the coastal wilderness nearby.

## One Week (7 Days) in Vancouver

### Days 1–2
Route: Downtown Waterfront, Stanley Park Seawall, West End, and Gastown. Start your morning at Canada Place, walking west along the Coal Harbour paved seawall toward Stanley Park. Loop counter-clockwise along the full nine-kilometer ocean perimeter to English Bay, then cut inland through the West End along Denman Street to finish the evening in historic Gastown.
Timing: Begin the seawall walk by 8:30 AM to beat the peak bicycle rental traffic. Reserve Gastown for 6:00 PM onward when dinner venues open.
Practical Warning: Gastown directly borders the Downtown Eastside (DTES), an area marked by high rates of homelessness and open drug use. Avoid walking east past Columbia Street along East Hastings Street, particularly after dark.

### Days 3–4
Route: Granville Island, Kitsilano, and Mount Pleasant. Catch an Aquabus ferry across False Creek to Granville Island Public Market. From there, trace the ocean trail west to Kitsilano Beach, then ride the 10th Avenue bike corridor or transit east into Mount Pleasant along the Main Street corridor.
Timing: Reach Granville Island by 9:00 AM before tour buses arrive. Spend mid-afternoon in Kitsilano, and target 5:30 PM for craft breweries along Mount Pleasant's "Brewery Creek."
Practical Warning: Vehicle parking near Granville Island and Kitsilano Beach is strictly enforced, scarce, and expensive. Use public transit, micro-ferries, or bikes rather than driving.

### Days 5–6
Route: North Shore (Lynn Canyon, Cleveland Dam, and Lonsdale Quay). Take the SeaBus ferry across Burrard Inlet from Waterfront Station. Transfer to local buses serving Lynn Canyon Park (for suspension bridge trails without the Capilano entry fee) and the Capilano River Regional Park near Cleveland Dam. Return south to the Shipyards District at Lonsdale Quay.
Timing: Allow full-day blocks from 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM. Trail light fades quickly under the dense canopy, even in late afternoon.
Practical Warning: North Shore mountain trails feature wet, slippery roots and steep granite steps. Waterproof footwear with aggressive tread is non-negotiable, and phone service drops out in deeper ravines.

### Day 7
Route: Richmond and Steveston Village. Take the Canada Line south to Richmond's Golden Village for an intensive dim sum tour, then take local bus 402 or 406 southwest to historic Steveston Village along the Fraser River mouth.
Timing: Plan to arrive for dim sum right at 10:00 AM. Spend 1:00 PM to 4:00 PM exploring Steveston's wooden boardwalks and historic cannery sites.
Practical Warning: Top-rated dim sum venues in Richmond operate on rigid reservation windows; walking in without a booking between 11:00 AM and 1:00 PM on weekends can result in wait times exceeding 90 minutes.

## Getting Around

Vancouver’s public transit network, operated by TransLink, is fast, clean, and comprehensive. You do not need to buy a physical transit card unless you want minor fare discounts; contactless credit cards, debit cards, and mobile wallets (Apple Pay/Google Pay) work directly at all SkyTrain faregates, SeaBus turnstiles, and bus readers. Tap the same card on entry and exit for trains and ferries, but tap only *on* when entering buses, as bus travel across the entire system is treated as a single zone. The SkyTrain connects Vancouver International Airport (YVR) directly to downtown in 26 minutes via the Canada Line.

## Local Tip

Vancouver’s tap water is among the cleanest municipal drinking water in North America, sourced directly from alpine rain and snowpack in the protected Capilano, Seymour, and Coquitlam watersheds; buy a reusable bottle and skip paying for bottled water anywhere in the city. Additionally, local alcohol laws permit public drinking only in designated municipal parks and select beaches between late spring and autumn. Look for clear city signage marking the legal boundary zones (such as sections of Kitsilano Beach Park and Dude Chilling Park). Purchase your craft beer at independent private beer stores rather than government BC Liquor stores, which shut down early on Sundays and holidays.
