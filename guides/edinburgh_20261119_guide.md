Edinburgh Neighborhood by Neighborhood
Edinburgh, the capital of Scotland, is a city that seamlessly blends history, culture, and natural beauty. For a first-time visitor, the city can seem overwhelming, with its cobblestone streets, historic landmarks, and vibrant cultural scene. However, with a little planning, visitors can experience the best of Edinburgh, from the stunning architecture of the Old Town to the trendy bars and restaurants of the New Town. The city is also surrounded by beautiful scenery, with the Pentland Hills to the south and the Firth of Forth to the north, offering plenty of opportunities for outdoor activities and exploration.

As a first-time visitor, it's a good idea to start in the city center, where many of the main attractions are within walking distance. The Royal Mile, which runs through the heart of the Old Town, is a great place to begin, with its street performers, shops, and historical landmarks like Edinburgh Castle and St. Giles' Cathedral. From there, visitors can explore the surrounding neighborhoods, each with its own unique character and charm. The New Town, with its Georgian architecture and upscale shops, is a great place to experience the city's more modern side, while the Water of Leith walkway offers a peaceful escape from the hustle and bustle of the city.

# Edinburgh Neighborhood by Neighborhood

## A Weekend in Edinburgh
### Day 1 (Saturday)
Start the day in the Old Town, where you can visit Edinburgh Castle and explore the Royal Mile. Begin at the top of the Royal Mile, at the castle, and work your way down, taking in the street performers and historical landmarks along the way. Be sure to visit St. Giles' Cathedral and the Scottish Storytelling Centre, and stop for lunch at one of the many traditional pubs or cafes. In the afternoon, head to the National Museum of Scotland, which is located just off the Royal Mile. One practical warning: be prepared for crowds and long lines at the castle and other popular attractions, especially during peak season.

### Day 2 (Sunday)
Spend the day exploring the New Town and the Water of Leith walkway. Start at the top of George Street, which is lined with upscale shops and restaurants, and work your way down to the Scottish National Gallery, which features a world-class collection of art. From there, head to the Water of Leith walkway, which follows the course of the river and offers a peaceful escape from the city. The walkway takes you through several of Edinburgh's neighborhoods, including the trendy Stockbridge area, which is known for its boutique shops and cafes. One practical warning: be aware of the weather, as the walkway can be muddy and slippery in wet conditions.

## Getting Around
The easiest way to get around Edinburgh is by using the city's public transportation system, which includes buses and trams. The city's local transit card, called a Ridacard, can be purchased at most newsagents or online, and offers unlimited travel on buses and trams for a fixed period of time. Alternatively, visitors can buy individual tickets or use a contactless payment card to pay for their fares.

Edinburgh has a vibrant food scene, with a wide range of options to suit all tastes and budgets. From traditional Scottish cuisine to fine dining and casual cafes, there's something for everyone. The Royal McGregor is a great place to try traditional Scottish dishes like haggis and neeps and tatties, while The Kitchin offers a more upscale dining experience. For a casual meal or snack, Lovecrumbs is a great option, with delicious cakes and sandwiches. The Beehive Inn is a cozy pub with a great selection of beers and pub grub.

## Local Tip
One thing that guidebooks often skip is the city's many hidden closes and courtyards, which are tucked away off the main streets. These hidden gems offer a glimpse into Edinburgh's past, with many of them dating back to the 17th and 18th centuries. The closes are often narrow and winding, with tall tenements looming above, and feature a range of interesting shops, bars, and restaurants. Some of the best closes to explore include Advocate's Close, which features a beautiful old church and a range of boutique shops, and Dunbar's Close, which is home to a number of trendy bars and restaurants. Visitors can spend hours exploring these hidden corners of the city, and discovering the unique character and charm of each one.
