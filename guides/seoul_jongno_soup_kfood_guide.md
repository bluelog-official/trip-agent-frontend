# Jongno Soup Route: Breakfast Broth Before the Palaces

Jongno is where a Seoul day should start if the palaces are the rest of the plan. The soup shops here open for people who work, not for people who want a brunch reservation. Eat a bowl, then walk to Gyeongbokgung or Changdeokgung with the meal already finished. Trying to sightsee first and eat at 13:30 puts you in the worst queue of the day, when the same shops are either closed for a break or packed with tour tables.

## The bowl that is the breakfast

### Seolleongtang, haejang, and what to skip

A milky seolleongtang is the default. It arrives plain. You add salt, scallion, and pepper at the table. That is not a missing ingredient. Rice is sometimes in the bowl and sometimes on the side. Eat it there. Wrapping it to go wastes the reason you sat down. Expect ₩9,000 to ₩13,000. A haejang-guk, the hangover stew with beef and bean sprouts, is the sharper alternative if you want chili in the morning. It is a different shop personality: louder, redder, and less useful if you are about to spend two hours in a quiet palace garden.

Do not order a set that adds barbecue before 11:00. You will not finish it, and you will not want lunch. Kimchi and kkakdugi come with the bowl. Asking for extra rice is normal. Asking the kitchen to make the soup less salty after you have already seasoned it is not.

## How the morning fits a palace

### Timing that keeps the ticket useful

Be inside the soup shop by 08:30 if you want a table without a wait. Palace opening is later. The gap is a walk up Samcheong-dong or a short subway hop to Gyeongbokgung, not a second cafe meal. Coffee after the bowl is optional and should be one drink you can carry. A dessert cafe with a queue is how the palace morning disappears.

If the shop you wanted has a line out the door, take the next seolleongtang sign on the same block. Jongno's advantage is density. Loyalty to one famous name is how you stand outside while the broth next door is ready.

## Lunch is a noodle, not another feast

After the palace, a kalguksu shop toward Insadong or Ikseon-dong is the second meal. Knife-cut noodles in anchovy or chicken broth run about ₩9,000 to ₩12,000. Share a pancake only if two people are still hungry. One person does not need three wheat dishes in six hours. Stop after the noodles if dinner will be anywhere else in the city. This route is a morning and a lunch, then you are free.

## Local tip

Many of these shops close in the mid-afternoon and do not reopen for a fashionable dinner. Read the hours on the door before you promise yourself the same bowl at 19:00. The breakfast pot is the one they care about. Cards work in most sit-down shops now, but a soup counter with a tray still prefers cash or a simple tap. Have both.
