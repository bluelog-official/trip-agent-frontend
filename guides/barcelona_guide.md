# Exploring Barcelona: A 4-Day Itinerary

Barcelona is a beach city and a Gothic city, and the two should not be scheduled as one sprint. This 4-day route gives Sagrada Família a timed morning, the Gothic Quarter a walking day, and Barceloneta a late afternoon rather than a commuting base.

## Barcelona in 4 Days, Neighborhood by Neighborhood

### Day 1: The Gothic Quarter and the Ramblas with a limit

Start at the cathedral when it opens and walk the small streets toward Plaça Sant Jaume and Plaça Reial. The quarter is the visit. La Rambla is a street you cross or walk once, not a promenade you owe an hour. The Boqueria is worth a pass if you are already there and willing to eat standing up away from the entrance juice stalls. Stay in the Eixample or near the Gothic Quarter. A beach hotel looks right until every monument is a bus ride in the heat.

### Day 2: Sagrada Família at your slot, then a planned garden

Book the basilica and enter at your time. A tower ticket is optional and separate. The interior is the reason to be there. Afterward, choose Park Güell only if you already have a timed entry and the energy for the hill. Otherwise walk the Eixample grid, look at one Casa Batlló or Casa Milà exterior, and stop paying for facades. Two Gaudí interiors in one day is plenty. Eat in the Eixample, where the lunch menus are calmer than the cathedral squares.

### Day 3: Gràcia, then the water

Gràcia is the neighborhood day: plazas, bakeries, and a walk that does not need a ticket. Go in the morning before the afternoon nap closes the small shops. Barceloneta is the late afternoon. Swim if the flags allow it, walk the promenade, and eat back from the first line of restaurants that face the sand. Montjuïc is the alternative if you want the view and the Miro foundation more than the beach. Do not add a Costa Brava day trip beside either plan.

### Day 4: A last pastry, then the airport train

The last morning is a coffee, a pastry, and a short revisit of one square you liked. The Aerobus or the airport metro reaches El Prat. Leave at least two and a half hours before an international flight if you are in the Eixample with luggage, and more if you are still in Gràcia. A beach morning plus a noon flight is the combination that misses the bag drop.

## Getting Around Barcelona

A T-Casual card covers the metro and buses for a short trip. The metro is the way between Sagrada Família, the Gothic Quarter, and Gràcia. Walking is the way inside each of those places. Taxis help with luggage and late nights. The beaches are a walk or a short metro from Barceloneta, not a reason to rent a car.

## Local Tip

The menu del día at lunch is the city's best-value seated meal, and it disappears in the evening. Eat a proper lunch, then let dinner be tapas or a smaller plate. A table on a cathedral square is a rent payment for the view, not a sign that the kitchen is better.
