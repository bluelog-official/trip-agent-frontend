# Stay Close in Rome for 4 Days

Rome is a walking city that becomes a queue if every ancient site is booked for the same afternoon. This 4-day route separates the Forum, the Vatican, and Trastevere, and it treats lunch as a neighborhood decision rather than a detour back to the hotel.

## Four Days in Rome

### Day 1: The Forum, the Colosseum, and a short Palatine

Book the Colosseum and Roman Forum as one timed morning. Enter when your slot opens and do the arena only if that extra ticket is already in hand. The Forum is the part visitors rush, and it is the part that explains the city. The Palatine is optional once your feet disagree. Walk out toward Piazza Venezia, look at the Vittoriano from the street, and stop. The afternoon is for a neighborhood: Monti for a slow lunch and a few streets, or the Jewish Ghetto if you want artichokes and a river loop. Do not add the Vatican to this day.

### Day 2: The Vatican at opening, then across the river

The Vatican Museums need the first entry of the day and a plan that ends at the Sistine Chapel without trying to see every gallery. St Peter's is a separate line. If you attend an early mass or arrive as the basilica opens, the interior is calmer than the midday cruise-ship hour. Climb the dome only if you still have stairs in you. Cross the river afterward and let Trastevere be the evening. The neighborhood is for dinner and a walk, not a list of churches after a museum morning.

### Day 3: A district that is still a city, not a set

Testaccio is the useful third day: the market in the morning, the neighborhood streets, and a long lunch. The Protestant Cemetery is a quiet hour if you want shade and a different Rome. Alternatively, take the morning for Villa Borghese and the Galleria Borghese, which requires a timed ticket booked ahead. Do not do both Testaccio and the Borghese and Trastevere again. One of them, done properly, is the day. A day trip to Pompeii or Tivoli replaces this day entirely and should not be squeezed beside it.

### Day 4: A fountain walk you already know, then the airport

The last morning can be the centro storico without pretending it is new: Pantheon, a coffee standing at the bar, Piazza Navona from the edge, and out. You will have passed these squares already. Seeing them without a ticket is the point. Fiumicino is the Leonardo Express from Termini, and Ciampino is a different bus. Leave at least three hours before an international flight, and do not plan a museum on the way to the station.

## Getting Around Rome

Walk the center. The metro is useful for the Vatican (Ottaviano) and for moving luggage to Termini, not for hopping between the Forum and Trastevere. A single taxi at arrival and departure is reasonable. Validate bus tickets if you use the surface lines. Cobblestones and a rolling suitcase are a poor match in Trastevere at night.

## Local Tip

Eat where the menu is short and the pasta is the point. A cacio e pepe or a supplì from a shop that is busy with Romans at 13:00 will beat a piazza table with a photograph of the dish. Stand at the coffee bar in the morning and sit down only when you are ready to pay for the chair.
