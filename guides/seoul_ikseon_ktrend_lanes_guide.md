# Ikseon Trend Lanes: Hanok Streets With a Closing Time

Ikseon-dong is a grid of small hanok lanes that now hold restaurants, bars, and shops. It photographs easily and clogs easily. The useful visit is a meal you reserved or arrived early for, a short walk that does not enter anyone's stairwell, and a leave time. The lanes are a neighborhood with a trend overlay. They are not a theme park that stays open for your itinerary.

## Book the table, then walk

### Lunch or an early dinner

The restaurants people talk about are small. A party of two that arrives at 19:00 on Saturday without a name on a list is a party that will stand in a lane. Reserve, or come for lunch when the same kitchens are calmer. A sit-down meal is often ₩15,000 to ₩30,000 a person before drinks. That range covers a modern Korean room and a pasta or wine bar that happens to be in a hanok. Read the menu outside. The building style does not tell you the price.

If you cannot get the famous table, eat somewhere with seats rather than collecting a rejection. The lanes reward being inside a room. They do not reward circling with a phone.

### The walk between courses, or instead of a second place

After the meal, walk one loop. Stay on the public lane. Do not sit on a stoop that is clearly a house entrance, and do not fly a drone or a gimbal at face height. Shops that sell small goods are fine to enter. A courtyard that is someone's yard is not an installation.

One drink after the meal is the trend version of the evening, ₩8,000 to ₩14,000, in a bar that posts the price. A second and third bar in the same grid is how you lose the exit. Jongno and Jongno 3-ga stations are close. Know which one is downhill from your restaurant before the drink.

## What to combine, and what collides

Ikseon sits near Insadong and the palaces. A morning palace and an Ikseon lunch is a good pair. A morning palace, a Gwangjang feast, and an Ikseon dinner is three meals and a lot of walking, and you should drop one. This page assumes Ikseon is the sit-down. Let the market be a different day if you want both at their best.

Rain changes the lanes. The brick is slick, umbrellas hit, and the rooms smell like wet coats. Still go if you have a reservation. Do not go only to photograph roofs you cannot see under a hood.

## Local tip

Save the restaurant's Korean name and the exact lane note from the booking message. Ride-share pins drop people on the main road, and the last two minutes are a walk the driver will not invent. If you are meeting someone, meet at the station exit first, then walk in together. Finding a friend inside the grid at dusk is slower than the meal.
