# Seoul Food Route: Markets, Alleys, and a Night Bowl

This Seoul food route stays north of the river and eats three times without turning the day into a taxi tour. Start at Gwangjang Market while the pancake stalls are still cooking for workers, walk the Ikseon-dong lanes for one sit-down meal, and finish with a late soup near Jongno. The point is not to collect famous signs. The point is to know which counter is a meal and which alley is only a queue.

## Morning market, before the tour groups

### Gwangjang, and how to order

Reach Gwangjang Market by 10:30. Cheonggyecheon or Jongno 5-ga puts you at the edge of the hall. Walk past the souvenir socks until you smell oil. A busy bindaetteok stall with a flat griddle is the breakfast. Point at the pancake, pay about ₩5,000 to ₩8,000, and eat it standing or at the narrow shared table. Mayak gimbap is the second bite, not a substitute: one roll is a snack, two rolls plus the pancake is a meal. If a staff member waves you toward a laminated English set menu at the entrance, keep walking. Those sets are priced for people who will not compare stalls.

Do not sit down for a full barbecue here in the morning. The market is for the griddle and the rice rolls. Save a table for later, when you actually want to stay an hour.

## Afternoon alley, one real table

### Ikseon-dong without the photo lap

Ikseon-dong is a ten-minute walk from the market if you use the small streets instead of returning to the main road. Pick one restaurant with a paper menu in the window and a lunch service that ends around 15:00. A kimchi jjigae or a soybean stew in this neighborhood is usually ₩9,000 to ₩14,000 before a bottle of soda. Order the stew and a bowl of rice. Skip the third side-dish upsell if the table is already full; the banchan that arrives with the stew is the meal.

The hanok lanes are photogenic and narrow. Walk on the left, do not stop in a doorway, and leave if a shop asks you to buy a drink before you take a picture of the wall. The food is inside the restaurants, not on the mural.

### What this route skips on purpose

Myeongdong street snacks can wait for another evening. They are a crowd, not a second lunch. Namdaemun is a different market with a different clock, and adding it makes the night bowl late. One market and one alley is the route that still has an appetite at 19:30.

## Night bowl near the hotel

Sleep in Jongno or Euljiro if you want the last meal on foot. A seolleongtang or kalguksu shop that opens for dinner, not only for breakfast, is the right ending. Budget ₩10,000 to ₩13,000 for the bowl. Eat it before 20:30 if the shop is a lunch-famous place that happens to stay open; the broth is better earlier. If your hotel is in Gangnam, take Line 2 or Line 3 back after the bowl instead of hunting for a second dinner in the hotel basement.

## Getting between the meals

T-money or a phone transit card covers the arrival subway. After that, this route is walking. Taxis are for rain or a late return south of the river, not for the gap between Gwangjang and Ikseon-dong. Wear shoes you can stand in. The market floor is wet near the grills.

## Local tip

Cash still moves faster at a market stall than a foreign card that needs a signature. Take ₩30,000 in small notes before you enter the hall. If a stall only takes transfer apps you do not have, choose the next griddle. There is always another pancake.
