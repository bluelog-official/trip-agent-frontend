# How to Spend 2 Days (Weekend) in Prague
![A stunning aerial view of Prague highlighting the city's bridges and historical architecture.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/16309117/pexels-photo-16309117.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)
*Photo by [Jarod Barton](https://www.pexels.com/@jarod)*

Prague, the capital of the Czech Republic, is one of Central Europe's most visually preserved cities, defined by gothic spires, cobblestone alleys, and a centuries-old pub culture. For a first-time visitor, its compact center makes it remarkably walkable, allowing you to move easily between medieval squares and hilltop castles without feeling rushed.

A two-day trip requires smart pacing to avoid dense tourist bottlenecks while still catching classic vistas over the Vltava River. Focus your limited time on distinct historic districts—Old Town and Malá Strana on Saturday, and Hradčany, Letná, and Karlín on Sunday—and embrace the slower side of local Czech dining once evening arrives.

## A Weekend in Prague

### Day 1 (Saturday)

*Route & Timing:* Start early at 08:30 in Old Town Square (Staroměstské náměstí) to see the Astronomical Clock chime before the main crowds arrive. Spend the morning wandering through the narrow lanes of the historic Jewish Quarter (Josefov). Around 12:00, walk across the iconic Charles Bridge (Karlův most) before it hits peak capacity. 

By afternoon (13:30–16:30), explore Malá Strana (Lesser Town), stopping by the Lennon Wall and strolling through Kampa Island along the Čertovka canal. Conclude your day at 17:30 by heading up to Petřín Hill via the funicular or walking trails for sunset views over the red tiled roofs of the city, followed by a relaxed dinner in Malá Strana.

*Practical Warning:* Avoid the currency exchange kiosks around Old Town Square and Karlova Street. Many list zero-percent commission in large text but apply predatory exchange rates that hide fees up to 28 percent. Stick to official bank ATMs or use contactless card payments, which are accepted almost everywhere.

### Day 2 (Sunday)

*Route & Timing:* Begin your morning at 08:30 up at the Prague Castle complex (Pražský hrad) in Hradčany to clear security before tour buses arrive. Take two to three hours (09:00–12:00) to visit St. Vitus Cathedral, the Old Royal Palace, and the Golden Lane. 

At 12:30, walk down through the vineyards of Saint Wenceslas toward Letná Park (Letenské sady). Enjoy a casual lunch and a beer at the Letná beer garden while taking in panoramic views of Prague's bridges. From 16:00 onwards, leave the heavy tourist center and take tram 8 or 24 into Karlín, a revamped industrial neighborhood, to explore its modern cafes, indie shops, and relaxed dinner spots.

*Practical Warning:* Do not purchase Prague Castle entrance tickets from street vendors claiming to offer "fast-track access." Mandatory security checkpoints managed by the police are required for every visitor regardless of ticket type, and official tickets should only be bought at the main castle ticket offices or through the official Prague Castle website.

## Getting Around

Prague has one of the cleanest, most efficient public transit systems in Europe, consisting of integrated metro lines, trams, and buses run by PID. You rarely need taxis. The simplest way to travel is to pay as you go using a contactless credit card or phone directly at the yellow validators installed inside every tram, bus, and metro station entrance. 

A 30-minute pass costs 30 CZK, while a 90-minute pass costs 40 CZK. If you plan to ride public transit frequently across 24 hours, buy a 120 CZK day ticket via the PID Lítačka mobile app or terminal machines. For the historic core (Old Town to Malá Strana), walking remains the fastest and most atmospheric mode of transport.

## Local Tip

Skip walking solely on the open main streets when traversing the New Town and Old Town areas; instead, use Prague's extensive network of covered passage systems (*pasáže*). Built in the early 20th century, passages like Pasáž Lucerna, Pasáž Světozor, and Pasáž Koruna allow you to cut through entire city blocks out of the rain and wind. They house hidden art installations—such as David Černý's upside-down horse hanging inside Lucerna—along with independent art-house cinemas, retro ice cream shops, and quiet espresso bars that standard street-level guidebooks rarely mention.
