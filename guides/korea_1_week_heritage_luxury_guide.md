# Korea: 1 Week of Hanok Lanes and a Quieter Luxury Stay

A week in Korea at a luxury pace is a few neighborhoods done slowly, plus one overnight outside Seoul. This route uses Bukchon and Ikseon-dong for the hanok lanes, Seongbuk-dong for a quiet afternoon, and a Jeonju night so the hanok village is a stay instead of a photo stop between palaces.

Luxury here means a reserved counter, a car for the Jeonju leg, and a hotel you return to in daylight. It does not mean five palaces and a department store every evening.

## Days 1–2: north of the river

Open with Gyeongbokgung at the gate time, then walk Bukchon as a neighborhood. Do not stand on a resident's step for a photo. Ikseon-dong is the narrower evening, with one booked Korean counter rather than a crawl.

Sleep in Jongno or a hanok stay you reserved by name. A Gangnam tower makes every morning a commute.

## Days 3–4: Seongbuk-dong and a modern afternoon

Seongbuk-dong is the quiet hill: a gallery or a small museum, then lunch with a view you did not queue an hour for. Keep Hongdae or Seongsu for day 4, and only one of them. The Han River park at sunset replaces a second neighborhood if the weather is clear.

## Days 5–6: Jeonju overnight

Take the train, not a day bus that turns around at dusk. Sleep in the hanok village or just outside it so the lanes are empty in the morning. Eat a proper Jeonju meal at a reserved room. The souvenir street is optional and short.

## Day 7: Seoul soup, then the airport

A last seolleongtang near the hotel, then AREX to Incheon. Leave three and a half hours before an international departure if you start north of the river with luggage. Gimpo is a different airport. Read the ticket.

## Getting around

Subway for Seoul days. A hired car or the KTX for Jeonju, booked with the hotel name in Korean. Taxis inside Bukchon are for the ride out with luggage, not for the lanes.
