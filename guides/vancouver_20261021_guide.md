What to Do in Vancouver for 1 Week

Vancouver is a city where the urban landscape bleeds seamlessly into the wild. As a first-time visitor, you will quickly notice that the pace of life here is dictated by the weather and the proximity to the Pacific Ocean. It is a place that rewards those who pack layers, appreciate craft coffee, and have a genuine interest in spending time outdoors, regardless of whether the skies are grey or blue.

The city is geographically compact, which makes it deceptive; you can easily spend an entire day getting lost in a single park or neighborhood. The key to enjoying your week here is to balance the high-energy tourist landmarks with the slower, quieter rhythms of the local residential pockets. Do not try to rush your itinerary; Vancouver is best experienced when you allow yourself the time to wander through the seawall or sit by the water with a book.

# What to Do in Vancouver for 1 Week

## One Week (7 Days) in Vancouver

### Days 1–2
Start your journey in the West End and Stanley Park. On the first day, rent a bicycle and ride the full 9-kilometer Stanley Park Seawall loop to get your bearings. Spend the second day exploring the beaches of English Bay and the dense, charming streets of the West End. 
Practical Warning: Do not attempt to walk the entire seawall in one go if you are wearing stiff footwear; the path is paved but deceptive in length, and your feet will regret it by the time you reach the park's far side.

### Days 3–4
Head across the water to the North Shore. Dedicate your third day to the Capilano Suspension Bridge or the free Lynn Canyon Park for a more rugged experience. On the fourth day, explore the Lonsdale Quay area in North Vancouver and take the SeaBus back to the downtown core. 
Practical Warning: The North Shore mountains act as a weather barrier; it is frequently raining in the canyons even when the sun is shining in downtown Vancouver, so always carry a lightweight waterproof shell.

### Days 5–6
Focus on the cultural hubs of Gastown and Mount Pleasant. Use day five to walk the cobblestone streets of Gastown, visiting the steam clock and local boutiques. On day six, head south to Main Street in Mount Pleasant to explore the city’s best independent galleries, vintage shops, and craft breweries. 
Practical Warning: Gastown is a beautiful historic area, but it borders the Downtown Eastside; stay alert and stick to the main thoroughfares like Water Street, especially after dark.

### Day 7
Spend your final day on Granville Island. Start early to beat the crowds at the Public Market, grab fresh fruit and local cheeses for a picnic, and spend the afternoon watching the buskers or taking a small ferry ride across False Creek. 
Practical Warning: Granville Island is not accessible by SkyTrain; you must take a bus or the Aquabus ferry, and parking on the island during peak summer weekends is an exercise in extreme patience.

## Getting Around

The simplest and most efficient way to navigate Vancouver is by using the Compass Card. This reloadable smart card works across all TransLink services, including the SkyTrain, the SeaBus, and the city’s extensive bus network. You can purchase a Compass Card at any SkyTrain station or major convenience store. If you prefer not to carry a physical card, the system now supports "tap-to-pay" using contactless credit cards or mobile wallets directly at the fare gates. For the best experience, download the Transit app, which provides real-time tracking for buses and trains, ensuring you aren't left waiting in the rain.

## Local Tip

Most guidebooks will tell you to visit the Vancouver Lookout for a view of the city, but locals know that the best view is actually found at the top of the Burrard Street Bridge or from the park benches at Queen Elizabeth Park. If you want a truly local perspective, head to the "hidden" elevated walkway near the Vancouver Public Library’s central branch. It offers a stunning architectural frame of the skyline without the ticket price. Furthermore, if you find yourself at a restaurant and the menu offers "Spot Prawns" during the short season in May, order them immediately; it is a point of local pride and a culinary highlight you won't find anywhere else in the world with the same freshness.
