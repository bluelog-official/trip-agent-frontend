# What to Do in Prague for 2 Days (Weekend)
![A stunning aerial view of Prague highlighting the city's bridges and historical architecture.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/16309117/pexels-photo-16309117.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)
*Photo by [Jarod Barton](https://www.pexels.com/@jarod)*

Prague is one of Europe's most walkable cities, where centuries of preserved Gothic, Renaissance, and Baroque architecture line the banks of the Vltava River. For first-time visitors, the sheer density of historic sights can feel overwhelming, but the compact geography means you can easily cover the core highlights without rushing if you structure your days logically.

To get the most out of a short stay, focus on exploring distinct neighborhoods on foot rather than bouncing across the river repeatedly. Expect cobblestone streets, heavy traditional cuisine, world-class beer culture, and thick crowds around major landmarks like the Charles Bridge, which are best navigated with careful timing.

## A Weekend in Prague

### Day 1 (Saturday): Old Town (Staré Město) and the Jewish Quarter (Josefov)

Route & Timing: Begin at 09:00 at Old Town Square to watch the Astronomical Clock strike on the hour before the mid-day tour groups arrive. Walk north through the historic Jewish Quarter, visiting the Old Jewish Cemetery and the Spanish Synagogue between 10:30 and 13:00. In the afternoon around 15:00, stroll down Karlova Street toward the river bank to catch the late sun illuminating the city's spires.
Practical Warning: Avoid changing cash at exchange booths along Karlova Street or Old Town Square; many charge hidden commission fees up to 28 percent or offer predatory rates. Use contactless card payments or withdraw cash from bank ATMs attached to major institutions like Česká spořitelna or Komerční banka.

**Lesser Town (Malá Strana) and Prague Castle (Hradčany)**

Route & Timing: Cross the Charles Bridge by 08:30 to experience the river crossing before heavy crowds gather. Head uphill into Malá Strana, following Nerudova Street to reach the Prague Castle complex by 10:00. Spend three hours exploring St. Vitus Cathedral, the Old Royal Palace, and Golden Lane. Descend through the St. Wenceslas Vineyard at 14:30 for panoramic city views, finishing with a coffee in Kampa Park around 16:00.
Practical Warning: Taxis hailed directly on the street around the Castle and Malá Strana frequently overcharge foreign visitors; stick to ride-hailing apps like Bolt or Uber, or rely on public transport.

### Day 2 (Sunday): Vyšehrad Fortress, Vinohrady, and Žižkov

Route & Timing: Head south-east of the center at 09:30 to explore Vyšehrad, a high hilltop fortress offering quiet green gardens and river views away from the crowded core. Around 12:30, walk into the residential Vinohrady district for lunch, then wander past art nouveau apartment blocks toward the Žižkov Television Tower by 15:30 to explore Prague's alternative side.
Practical Warning: Watch out for uneven, original cobblestones throughout these hillside neighborhoods; wearing flimsy sandals or thin sneakers often leads to twisted ankles or extreme foot fatigue.

**New Town (Nové Město) and Riverside Promenade**

Route & Timing: Start at 09:00 at Wenceslas Square, taking in the National Museum and learning about modern Czech history. Walk west along Národní street toward the Náplavka riverbank promenade by 11:30 for local coffee and street food snacks. Wrap up your afternoon at 14:00 with a visit to the Dancing House and a quiet craft beer overlooking the Vltava before your departure.
Practical Warning: Stand on the right side of escalators in the Prague Metro system; locals walk up and down on the left side and will firmly push past anyone blocking the passage.

## Getting Around

Prague boasts one of the most efficient and reliable public transport networks in Europe, comprising integrated metro lines, trams, and buses. The simplest way to travel is by buying a 24-hour or 72-hour transit pass via the official PID Lítačka mobile app or using contactless ticket machines installed inside every tram and metro station. A single 30-minute ticket costs 30 CZK, while a 24-hour pass costs 120 CZK, giving you unlimited transfers across all transport modes within central zones. Always validate paper tickets in the yellow stamping machines upon entering metro stations or stepping onto trams, as plainclothes ticket inspectors frequently check tourists and issue non-negotiable cash fines on the spot.

## Local Tip

While most tourists head directly to crowded pubs near Charles Bridge for beer, locals evaluate pubs based on how well the tapster handles the pour. Czech beer culture is defined by the thick, wet head of foam that keeps the Pilsner Urquell fresh and carbonated. When ordering draft beer at a traditional hall, ask for a "hladinka" for a standard pour with three fingers of dense foam, or try a "šnyt" for a smaller beer poured into a large glass with half foam—it is the traditional way locals enjoy a quick, refreshing drink before moving on to dinner without getting overly full.
