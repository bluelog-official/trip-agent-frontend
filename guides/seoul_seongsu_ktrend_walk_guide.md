# Seongsu Trend Walk: Pop-up, Record, Coffee, Done

Seongsu's trend walk is a warehouse district that became cafes, pop-ups, and shops that change their paint every season. The neighborhood rewards a short list. See one pop-up that is actually open, buy or browse one object in a record or poster shop, drink one coffee outside a queue, and leave before the streets turn into a slow shuffle. Chasing every new logo is how you photograph doors and miss the city.

## Check the dates before you ride Line 2

### A pop-up is a temporary room

Pop-ups close. A blog post from last month is not a schedule. Look up the current installation the morning you go, including the last entry time. If it is dark, do not wait at the shutter for a restock that is not coming. Move to the record shop and the coffee. The neighborhood is still worth the subway when the headline room is shut, as long as you did not build the whole day on that one door.

When it is open, decide in advance whether you are there to look or to buy. Entry is sometimes free and sometimes tied to a purchase or a time slot. A ₩10,000 to ₩30,000 object you understand is a fair spend. A limited cup you must carry all afternoon is not, unless you live nearby.

### The coffee rule

If the cafe line leaves the building, pick another cafe. Seongsu has more seats than the most photographed one. A hand-drip or a simple latte is ₩5,000 to ₩8,000. Drink it, then walk toward Seoul Forest if you want trees, or back to the station if you are finished. Adding a second pop-up in Gangnam splits the day into traffic.

## Eat like a person who will walk

Lunch before the shops. A bakery line is not lunch. Noodles, a rice plate, or a sandwich you can name is ₩10,000 to ₩16,000 around the station. Then do the three stops. Then stop. Evening plans in Hongdae or a hotel north of the river are easier if you are not still in Seongsu at sunset holding two paper bags and a melting dessert.

## Local tip

Wear shoes for concrete and slight hills, and bring a tote that folds. Seongsu punishes a rolling suitcase and a hard-shell shopping plan. You are walking between warehouses, not between hotel doors. If a shop floor is a working studio with a rope, the rope is the exhibit boundary.
