# Inside Madrid: 4 Days Worth Planning
![Madrid travel](https://images.pexels.com/photos/346885/pexels-photo-346885.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600)
*Photo by [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com)*

Madrid does not reveal itself through iconic monuments alone. Its draw lies in the space between sights: late afternoon light hitting limestone facades, the clash of coffee spoons at stainless-steel bars, and neighborhoods that maintain distinct characters within a few blocks of one another. First-time visitors often rush between major art galleries, missing the subtle rhythm that makes the Spanish capital run.

To experience the city properly, adjust your clock immediately. Mornings start late, afternoon meals linger for two hours, and the streets stay active well past midnight. Pacing yourself is essential; trying to operate on a northern European schedule will leave you facing locked doors at dinner and empty plazas in the morning.

## 4 Days in Madrid

### Day 1
Neighborhood Route: Centro Histórico, Los Austrias, and Argüelles. Start at Puerta del Sol and walk west through Plaza Mayor toward the Royal Palace and Sabatini Gardens. Continue north along Calle Bailén to end your afternoon at the Temple of Debod for elevated views over the Casa de Campo park.

Timing: Begin at 09:30 before tour groups congest Plaza Mayor. Aim to finish the Royal Palace by 13:00. Take your main meal between 14:00 and 16:00, then resume walking around 18:00.

Practical Warning: Ticket queues at the Royal Palace routinely exceed an hour by mid-morning; book online timed-entry slots days ahead and ignore unauthorized ticket sellers surrounding the plaza.

### Day 2
Neighborhood Route: Paseo del Prado, Jerónimos, and Retiro. Dedicate your morning to the Prado Museum. Afterward, walk through the Jerónimos district to El Retiro Park, visiting the Glass Palace and the boating lake. End your day at the Reina Sofía Museum in the late afternoon.

Timing: Arrive at the Prado by 09:45 for a 10:00 entry. Spend three hours inside, followed by a light lunch at 13:30. Enter Retiro Park by 15:00 and head to Reina Sofía by 18:00.

Practical Warning: Retiro Park forcibly closes its gates during high wind alerts or heavy rain; check municipal government updates on storm days to avoid walking to a locked perimeter.

### Day 3
Neighborhood Route: Malasaña, Chueca, and Gran Vía. Explore the independent boutiques along Calle Fuencarral and the residential squares of Malasaña, such as Plaza Dos de Mayo. Cross Calle de Hortaleza into Chueca for vibrant street life, ending the day on the elevated terraces overlooking Gran Vía.

Timing: Do not start before 11:00, as shops in Malasaña rarely open earlier. Walk until 14:30, pause for lunch, and explore Chueca from 17:30 onward.

Practical Warning: Pickpocketing is highly concentrated along Gran Vía and inside the Sol transit station; keep zip closures facing inward on public escalators and never leave smartphones unattended on outdoor tables.

### Day 4
Neighborhood Route: Barrio de las Letras, La Latina, and Lavapiés. Trace the literary history of Calle Huertas, then head west into La Latina’s historic alleys along Calle Cava Baja. In the afternoon, descend into the multicultural neighborhood of Lavapiés, visiting the converted market halls of Mercado de San Fernando.

Timing: Begin at 10:00 around Calle Huertas. Settle into La Latina for long tapas plates starting at 13:30. Transition to Lavapiés around 17:30 for evening drinks.

Practical Warning: Many historic, family-run taverns in La Latina close on Mondays or Tuesdays; confirm specific opening days if your fourth day falls early in the work week.

## Getting Around

The simplest way to navigate Madrid is on foot, complemented by the Metro network for longer cross-city trips. Skip single-journey paper tickets and purchase a red "Tarjeta Multi" from any station ticket machine for €2.50. You can load this card with a 10-Journey ticket (Metrobús), valid for up to 10 rides across Metro Zone A and EMT city bus networks. Multiple travelers can share a single card by tapping it successively at turnstiles. Contactless bank cards are accepted directly at EMT bus readers, but the 10-journey loaded Multi card remains the most economical choice for several days of sightseeing.

## Local Tip

Beware the kitchen dead zone between 17:00 and 20:30. Restaurant kitchens in Madrid close strictly after afternoon lunch service ends around 16:30 and do not reopen for dinner until at least 20:30. Venues offering full hot meals at 18:00 are almost exclusively tourist-oriented spots relying on reheated frozen dishes. To bridge this gap like a local, adopt the habit of "tardeo"—order a small beer (caña) or draught vermouth (vermut de grifo) at a neighborhood bar, which will come with a small complimentary plate of olives, potato chips, or cured meat to sustain you until proper dinner hours.
