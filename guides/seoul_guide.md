# Seoul for Travelers Who Walk

Seoul is dense enough to walk and large enough to waste a day on transfers. This 4-day route pairs the palaces with Bukchon, gives Gwangjang Market a real meal, and saves Hongdae or the Han River for a night that does not also include a palace.

## Day by Day: 4 Days in Seoul

### Day 1: Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon, and Insadong

Reach Gyeongbokgung at opening. The palace is the morning, and the changing of the guard is optional theater, not the reason to be there. Walk out into Bukchon and treat the hanok lanes as a neighborhood, not a photo platform in front of someone's door. Insadong is the afternoon if you want tea and paper shops. Ikseon-dong is the narrower alternative. Sleep north of the river, in Jongno or near Insadong, if the first two days are the palaces. A Gangnam hotel makes every morning a subway project.

### Day 2: Changdeokgung, then a food market

Changdeokgung and the Secret Garden need a timed look, and they are quieter than the main palace if you go early. Do not add a third palace. After lunch, go to Gwangjang Market and eat there: bindaetteok, mayak gimbap, and one stall you choose because it is busy, not because a sign is in English. The market is a meal, not a souvenir aisle you rush through on the way to Myeongdong. Myeongdong can be a short evening loop for street food if you still want a crowd.

### Day 3: A modern neighborhood and the river

Use the day for either Hongdae and Yeonnam, or Seongsu, or a Han River park at sunset. Hongdae is the easier first choice: small shops, a long cafe afternoon, and music after dark. The river parks are the better sunset if the weather is clear. Namsan Tower is a night view with a cable car or a hike, and it replaces the neighborhood if you choose it. Do not try to add the DMZ to this day. The DMZ is a separate booked tour and a full morning.

### Day 4: A last soup, then Incheon

Seoul mornings are built for soup. A seolleongtang or a dumpling shop near the hotel is the right ending. The AREX train is the direct ride to Incheon. Leave at least three and a half hours before an international departure if you are starting north of the river with luggage. Gimpo is a different, closer airport used by many regional flights. Read the ticket before you memorize one station.

## Getting Around Seoul

Get a T-money card or use a transit card on your phone. The subway is the city. Buses fill the gaps and are harder to read on a first visit. Palaces, Bukchon, and Insadong are a walk once you are at Anguk or Gyeongbokgung station. Taxis are inexpensive for a late return and unnecessary at noon.

## Local Tip

Many of the meals people remember in Seoul are ordered at a machine or a counter, then eaten at a shared table. Do not wait for a host to seat you in a market stall. Point, pay, and sit. The palace districts empty of good food after 14:00, so eat before you start the long walk back.
