# The 4-Day Madrid Edit
![Low angle view of classical architecture with sculptures in Madrid, Spain.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/12039244/pexels-photo-12039244.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)
*Photo by [Julio Irrazabal](https://www.pexels.com/@julio-irrazabal-226640504)*

Madrid does not rely on a single marquee landmark to justify its reputation. Instead, the Spanish capital pulls you in through its urban atmosphere: high-altitude sunlight filtering down wide boulevards, public squares that function as neighborhood living rooms, and an unapologetic commitment to eating and living well.

For a first-time visitor, four days provides the ideal frame. It offers enough time to absorb the heavyweights—the Royal Palace and the Golden Triangle of Art—while leaving ample room to adapt to the city’s late-running internal clock, where lunches span hours and dinner rarely starts before nightfall.

## A 4-Day Route through Madrid

### Day 1: Historic Center and Royal Legacy

* Neighborhood Route: Begin at the bustling Puerta del Sol, walking west down Calle Mayor into the historic Plaza Mayor. From there, head toward the Royal Palace of Madrid and the adjoining Sabatini Gardens, concluding your evening with a tapas walk through the narrow stone lanes of La Latina, particularly along Calle Cava Baja.
* Timing: 09:00 to 22:00. Reserve the Royal Palace interior for 10:30; break for lunch at 14:00; begin evening dining after 20:30.
* Practical Warning: The stretch between Puerta del Sol and Plaza Mayor is a primary operational hub for opportunistic pickpockets; keep your belongings zipped and secured in front of you.

### Day 2: The Golden Triangle of Art and Retiro Park

* Neighborhood Route: Spend your morning along the tree-lined Paseo del Prado visiting the Prado Museum. Afterward, cross into the Barrio de las Letras (Literary Quarter) for lunch, then spend the late afternoon strolling through El Retiro Park to see the Palacio de Cristal and the boating lake.
* Timing: 09:30 to 21:00. Enter the Prado right at its morning opening; transition to the park by 16:00 when the light softens.
* Practical Warning: The main general-admission ticket line at the Prado can easily eat up two hours of your morning; pre-book a timed-entry ticket online at least several days in advance.

### Day 3: Modern Masters and Multicultural Streets

* Neighborhood Route: Dedicate your morning to 20th-century art at the Reina Sofía Museum to view Picasso’s *Guernica*. Walk south into Lavapiés to explore its steep hills, street art, and global food scene, before heading up to the Círculo de Bellas Artes rooftop terrace for sunset views over Gran Vía.
* Timing: 10:00 to 22:30. Spend 2.5 hours at Reina Sofía, head to Lavapiés for lunch by 14:30, and reach the rooftop by 19:30.
* Practical Warning: Lavapiés features steep inclines and worn, uneven cobblestones; avoid wearing thin flat shoes or unsupportive footwear today.

### Day 4: Upscale Shopping and Alternative Quarters

* Neighborhood Route:
 Explore the grid-like streets of the upscale Salamanca district along Calle Serrano and Mercado de la Paz. In the afternoon, cross the Castellana boulevard into the trendy, boutique-filled streets of Chueca and Malasaña, finishing along Calle Fuencarral.
* Timing: 10:30 to 23:00. Salamanca shopping in the morning; Malasaña cafes and cocktail bars from 17:00 onwards.
* Practical Warning: Independent boutiques and family-owned establishments in Malasaña often close for *siesta* between 14:00 and 17:00; time your shopping for late afternoon.

## Getting Around

Madrid’s public transportation network is clean, efficient, and cheap. Skip taxi queues at the airport or train stations and head directly to the Metro service machines. 

The simplest option for a tourist is to buy a physical Multi Card (*Tarjeta Multi*) for €2.50 from any red ticket machine at a metro station, then load it with a standard 10-Journey Ticket (*10-Viajes*). This pass costs roughly €6.10 to €12.20 depending on current regional transit subsidies, covers both the underground Metro and blue EMT city buses within Zone A, and can be tapped multiple times for several people traveling together. 

While central neighborhoods like Barrio de las Letras and Malasaña are deeply pedestrian-friendly, using Metro Line 1 (Blue) and Line 2 (Red) will significantly save your feet when moving across the city center.

## Local Tip

Resist the temptation to eat dinner before 20:30, no matter how hungry you feel after walking all day. Restaurants that open their kitchens for full dinner service at 19:00 are almost exclusively targeting tourists, often serving lower-quality, reheated items at inflated prices. Instead, adopt the local buffer strategy: around 18:30 or 19:00, step into a neighborhood *taberna*, order a small draft beer (*caña*) or a draught vermouth (*vermut de grifo*), and enjoy the small complimentary plate of olives, cured cheese, or potato omelet that comes with it. This traditional aperitif bridge will comfortably carry you through until authentic kitchens open their doors at 21:00.
