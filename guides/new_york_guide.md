# First Time in New York: 5 Days

New York is a walking city that punishes a plan made of scattered landmarks. This 5-day route keeps Lower Manhattan, Midtown, a museum morning, and one Brooklyn neighborhood in an order that follows the subway instead of fighting it.

## 5 Days in New York

### Day 1: Lower Manhattan and the waterfront

Start at the Staten Island Ferry if you want the Statue of Liberty without a pedestal ticket. The ride is free and the view is the point. Walk north through Battery Park and the 9/11 Memorial, then into the Financial District streets that still feel like a weekday even on Saturday. The Brooklyn Bridge is the late-afternoon walk, toward Brooklyn if you want sunset on the boardwalk side, toward Manhattan if you are already tired and the hotel is uptown. Eat downtown. Crossing back to Midtown just for dinner wastes the hour you saved by walking.

### Day 2: One museum, then Central Park with a boundary

Pick the Met or MoMA, not both. The Met is a morning that starts at opening and ends when you are hungry, not when you have seen the map. Enter from Fifth Avenue, choose two wings, and leave through the park side. Walk the Mall and Bethesda Terrace, then stop. The park is not a through-route to the Upper West Side unless that is where you are eating. A numbered-avenue walk, Madison or Columbus, is a better afternoon than a second museum. Times Square can be a ten-minute evening errand on the way to a theater, not a destination you build the day around.

### Day 3: The High Line into Chelsea, then a borough

Enter the High Line from the south end near Gansevoort and walk north until you are ready to step off into Chelsea. The elevated park is a path, not a place to spend the whole afternoon. From there, take the subway to one Brooklyn neighborhood and stay: Williamsburg if you want the waterfront and shops, or Carroll Gardens if you want a quieter lunch street. Do not try to 'see Brooklyn' from a bus window. One neighborhood done slowly is the visit.

### Day 4: A deli or a market, then the airport

The last morning should be a meal and a short walk. Katz's is a Lower East Side commitment with a line. A bagel on the Upper West Side or a market stall at Essex is the lighter version.

### Day 5: Departure

For JFK, leave Manhattan at least three hours before an international flight and use the AirTrain at the end of the subway or LIRR ride. Newark is a NJ Transit plus AirTrain problem, not a yellow-taxi default with luggage. LaGuardia is the shorter car ride and still needs a buffer in the afternoon.

## Getting Around New York

OMNY lets you tap a contactless card or phone on the subway and most buses. You do not need a separate MetroCard for a short trip. The subway is the trip. A taxi is for late night, bad weather, or a group with luggage. Walk the avenues in one direction and change streets only when you have a reason.

## Local Tip

New York restaurants that are worth the reputation often take a walk-in only at the bar, or a reservation that disappeared two weeks ago. Eat one famous place at opening, and let the other meals be a slice, a market, or a counter with a posted menu. The city is more generous at lunch than at 20:00.
