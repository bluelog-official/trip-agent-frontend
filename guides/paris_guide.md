# Paris With Time to Spare (4 Days)

Paris rewards a visitor who picks a bank of the river and stays there until evening. This 4-day guide keeps the Louvre, the Marais, the islands, and Montmartre on separate days so you are not crossing the city twice between museums.

## Paris in 4 Days, Neighborhood by Neighborhood

### Day 1: The islands, Notre-Dame, and the Marais

Start on Île de la Cité. The cathedral exterior and the square are the morning; a full interior slot is worth it only if you already hold a timed entry. Cross to Île Saint-Louis for a short walk, not a long cafe sit, then enter the Marais through the Place des Vosges. The arcades are the point. Keep the afternoon for the Jewish quarter around Rue des Rosiers and one small museum, the Picasso or the Carnavalet, rather than both. Sleep in the Marais, Saint-Germain, or near République so Day 2 does not begin with a long metro.

### Day 2: The Louvre with a hard stop, then the Seine

Enter the Louvre at opening with a timed ticket and a short list: the Denon wing for the large Italian rooms, then one other wing, then out. A first visit that tries to 'do the Louvre' loses the rest of the day in the Richelieu stairwells. Exit toward the Tuileries, walk to Place de la Concorde, and cross the river. Late afternoon is for the Musée d'Orsay only if you still have energy and a second timed ticket. Otherwise walk the Left Bank quays toward Saint-Germain and eat there. The Eiffel Tower looks better from the Trocadéro or a bridge at dusk than from a summit queue you did not book.

### Day 3: Montmartre early, then a neighborhood you did not schedule

Be above Abbesses before the portrait artists set up. Sacré-Cœur is a view and a church, not a half-day museum. Walk down through the vineyard side streets rather than the main stair that faces the tour buses. After lunch, choose one unfinished district: Canal Saint-Martin if you want a flat walk and bookshops, or the covered passages near the Grands Boulevards if the weather turns. Do not add Versailles to this day. Versailles is a separate morning with its own ticket and a train from Montparnasse or Saint-Lazare, and it erases Paris if you glue it onto Montmartre.

### Day 4: A market morning and the airport train

Use the last morning for food, not a new monument. Rue Cler, the Marché d'Aligre, or the Mouffetard street market will fill two hours if you are willing to eat standing up. Leave for Charles de Gaulle at least three hours before an international departure. The RER B is direct and crowded; a pre-booked taxi is the calmer choice with luggage. Orly is a different train. Check which airport is on the ticket before you memorize one route.

## Getting Around Paris

A carnet of metro tickets, or a phone ticket in the Île-de-France app, covers the city. The metro is faster than the bus for anything that crosses the river, and the bus is better when you want to see the street you are skipping. Validate every ticket. Walking is the right mode inside the Marais, on the islands, and in Montmartre once you are already up the hill.

## Local Tip

Dinner reservations in Paris matter more than breakfast reservations. Book one seated dinner a day and let lunch be a bakery, a market stall, or a bouillon where you queue once and sit at a shared table. The city is more enjoyable when you are not hunting a table at 20:30.
