# Vancouver Trip Notes for 1 Week
![Stunning view of Vancouver cityscape with colorful sunset sky reflecting on harbor water.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/18604163/pexels-photo-18604163.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Vancouver sits squeezed between the Salish Sea and the Pacific Coast Mountains. For a first-time visitor, the main draw is how quickly dense urban neighborhoods yield to temperate rainforests, ocean beaches, and alpine trails. It is a city best explored outdoors, but its distinct micro-districts—from historic Gastown to coastal Kitsilano—demand dedicated footwork.

Weather dictates daily life here. Rain can settle in for consecutive days, yet when the skies clear, few cities match its physical beauty. Plan your itinerary around flexible outdoorsy mornings and cozy indoor afternoons, keeping in mind that Vancouver operates on a relaxed, early-to-bed schedule compared to other major coastal hubs.

## One Week (7 Days) in Vancouver

### Days 1–2
Route: Start along the Seawall at Coal Harbour, loop around Stanley Park past Brockton Point, cut through Downtown along Robson Street, and finish in Gastown for evening dining.
Timing: Morning Seawall walk or cycle (09:00–12:30), afternoon Downtown wandering (13:30–16:30), late afternoon to evening in Gastown (17:00 onwards).
Practical Warning: Avoid walking south down East Hastings Street past Columbia Street when exiting Gastown; the Downtown Eastside begins abruptly there and can be uncomfortable for disoriented visitors.

### Days 3–4
Route: Take a micro-ferry across False Creek to Granville Island Public Market, walk along the coastal path west to Kitsilano Beach, and finish along West 4th Avenue.
Timing: Granville Island Market (08:30–11:30), coastal walk to Kits Beach (12:00–14:30), West 4th Avenue retail and dining (15:00–18:30).
Practical Warning: Parking near Kitsilano Beach on sunny days is virtually impossible; rely entirely on transit, walking, or bike paths to reach this corridor.

### Days 5–6
Route: Travel north over the inlet to North Vancouver. Visit Lynn Canyon Park for rainforest trails, stop at Lonsdale Quay, and wrap up at Deep Cove.
Timing: Morning Lynn Canyon hike (09:00–12:00), lunch at Lonsdale Quay Market (12:30–14:00), late afternoon walk or kayaking at Deep Cove (14:30–18:00).
Practical Warning: Traffic across the Lions Gate Bridge and Ironworkers Memorial Bridge bottlenecks heavily during weekday rush hours (15:30–18:30); cross early or use the SeaBus to avoid sitting in multi-hour jams.

### Day 7
Route: Explore the independent shops and coffee roasters of Mount Pleasant along Main Street, then cross over to Commercial Drive for vintage boutiques and Italian delis.
Timing: Main Street browsing and coffee (10:00–13:00), afternoon walk and food tasting along Commercial Drive (13:30–17:00).
Practical Warning: Independent storefronts on Main Street and Commercial Drive rarely open before 11:00 AM, so starting your retail walk too early will leave you facing closed doors.

## Getting Around

Vancouver features an efficient integrated transit system operated by TransLink, comprising the SkyTrain light rail, SeaBus ferries, and extensive bus routes. Visitors do not need to purchase physical transit cards anymore; simply tap any contactless credit card or mobile wallet directly at SkyTrain fare gates and on buses. For single-zone bus rides, tap in only, but remember to tap out on SkyTrain and SeaBus journeys to ensure you are charged the correct fare. For quick trips across False Creek, use the small blue-and-white Aquabus or False Creek Ferries, which take cash or card directly on board.

## Local Tip

Guidebooks often forget to mention that Vancouver's tap water comes directly from pristine, protected mountain reservoirs in the North Shore watershed. The quality is exceptional, making paid bottled water entirely redundant everywhere in the city. Bring a sturdy refillable bottle and fill it at any tap or public fountain. Additionally, take advantage of the local "Happy Hour" culture (typically running from 3:00 PM to 5:00 PM); it is the best way to sample high-end seafood and craft beer at half the usual dinner price without needing reservations weeks in advance.
