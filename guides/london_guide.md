# 5 Days in London: A Slower Route

London looks compact on a Tube map and very large on foot. This 5-day route stays near the Thames for the monuments, gives one morning to a free museum, and spends a market day in one neighborhood instead of sampling five.

## 5 Days in London

### Day 1: South Bank to the Tower

Start at Westminster Bridge only long enough to place the river, then walk the South Bank toward Tate Modern. The gallery is free and the turbine hall tells you quickly whether you want an hour inside. Cross the Millennium Bridge, pass St Paul's from the churchyard, and continue to Borough Market if you still need lunch. The Tower of London is a half-day with a paid ticket. Do it on this afternoon or skip it. Trying to add the London Eye as a third ticket turns the walk into a queue.

### Day 2: The British Museum, then a park with a gate

The British Museum is free and too large for a wander without a list. Choose two collections, start at opening, and leave by lunch. The Russell Square side is the calmer exit. Walk to Covent Garden only if you want the street, not the piazza performers. Regent's Park or St James's Park is the better second half: a defined gate, a loop, and a way back to the Tube. Book one West End play for the evening and eat near the theater. A pre-show dinner across the city is how people miss the curtain.

### Day 3: A neighborhood that is not a monument

Pick Notting Hill and Portobello if you want a market street, or Greenwich if you want the river and a hill, or Hampstead if you want the heath. Do not chain them. London neighborhoods are the trip once the monuments are done, and they are ruined by a schedule that treats them as stops between stations. If the weather is clear, the Greenwich hill view is the one that explains the river. If it is raining, a covered market and a long lunch is the honest plan.

### Day 4: Breakfast, a short gallery, the airport train

Use the last morning for a bakery and one free room in the National Gallery if you are already near Trafalgar Square. Heathrow is the Elizabeth line or the Piccadilly line; the Elizabeth line is the one to choose with luggage. Gatwick is a dedicated train from Victoria or London Bridge, not the Tube.

### Day 5: Departure

Leave at least three hours before an international departure, and earlier if you still need to buy the airport ticket at a machine.

## Getting Around London

Tap a contactless card or phone on the Tube, the Elizabeth line, the Overground, and most buses. Daily caps make a separate travelcard unnecessary for a short visit. The Tube is for distance. Walking is for the South Bank, the City lanes, and any neighborhood you came to see. A black cab is a late-night tool, not the daytime plan.

## Local Tip

Free museums are the budget of the trip, and they are also the trap. One great museum in the morning is worth more than three foyers. Eat a market lunch so the afternoon can be a park or a play, not another ticket.
