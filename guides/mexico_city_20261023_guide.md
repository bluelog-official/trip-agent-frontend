# See Mexico City in 5 Days
![A stunning aerial view of Chapultepec Park with cityscape and mountains in the background.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/28577135/pexels-photo-28577135.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)
*Photo by [Erhart Fabian Castillo Castellanos](https://www.pexels.com/@erhart-fabian-castillo-castellanos-179012)*

Mexico City sits at an altitude of 2,240 meters, sprawling across a mountain-ringed valley that was once a network of Aztec lakes. For a first-time visitor, the sheer scale of the Mexican capital can feel intimidating, but its urban layout functions like a cluster of distinct villages. Each neighborhood, or colonia, carries its own architectural rhythm, culinary focus, and daily pace.

Rather than trying to cross the entire city multiple times a day through dense traffic, the most practical approach is to explore one defined zone at a time. Balancing high-intensity historic sites with calmer, tree-lined residential neighborhoods allows you to absorb the city's energy without running into transit fatigue.

## 5 Days in Mexico City

### Day 1
Neighborhood Route: Begin at the vast Zócalo plaza in Centro Histórico, step inside the Metropolitan Cathedral, and examine the active archaeological excavations at Templo Mayor. Walk west down the pedestrianized Calle Madero toward the tiled facade of Casa de los Azulejos, finishing at Palacio de Bellas Artes and Alameda Central park.
Timing: 08:30 to 17:00.
Practical Warning: Calle Moneda and the streets east of the Zócalo get extremely congested with foot traffic and vendors; carry backpacks across your chest and keep mobile phones secured.

### Day 2
Neighborhood Route: Start early in Coyoacán at Frida Kahlo’s Casa Azul, then stroll through Plaza Hidalgo to Mercado de Coyoacán for an early lunch. In the afternoon, head west to the quiet cobbled streets, colonial courtyards, and plazas of neighboring San Ángel.
Timing: 09:00 to 18:00.
Practical Warning: Tickets for the Frida Kahlo Museum must be purchased online weeks in advance for a strict timeslot; walk-up tickets are not sold at the door.

### Day 3
Neighborhood Route: Walk up the hill inside Bosque de Chapultepec to tour Chapultepec Castle. Spend the midday hours inside the nearby National Museum of Anthropology, then exit northward into Polanco for a late lunch along Avenida Campos Elíseos.
Timing: 09:00 to 18:30.
Practical Warning: The Anthropology Museum spans 23 exhibition halls; trying to visit every room causes severe museum fatigue, so focus your time on the central Mexica and Maya halls.

### Day 4
Neighborhood Route: Take a morning walk around Avenida Ámsterdam in Condesa, pausing in Parque México and Parque España. Move east into Roma Norte along Calle Colima to browse independent art galleries, coffee roasters, and mid-century design boutiques.
Timing: 10:00 to 20:00.
Practical Warning: Decades of seismic activity and mature tree roots have rendered the sidewalks throughout Roma and Condesa notoriously uneven; watch your footing to avoid trips and sprained ankles.

### Day 5
Neighborhood Route: Travel south early in the morning to the ecological reserve piers of Xochimilco for a quiet boat ride along the ancient canal system. Return north to Colonia Juárez for late afternoon coffee, shopping along Calle Dinamarca, and dinner near Zona Rosa.
Timing: 07:30 to 19:00.
Practical Warning: At Xochimilco piers, official rates for trajinera boat rentals are set per boat per hour by the city government, not per passenger; confirm the official rate sheet at the dock kiosk before boarding.

## Getting Around

The standard way to travel through Mexico City is using the Tarjeta de Movilidad Integrada (Integrated Mobility Card). Available at Metro and Metrobús station vending machines for 15 MXN, this single contactless card accepts recharges and grants access to the Metro subway system, the surface Metrobús lines, urban buses, and the Cablebús aerial cable cars. Fares are remarkably low, ranging from 5 MXN for the Metro to 7 MXN for the Metrobús.

For late-night travel, trips through unfamiliar outer districts, or journeys during heavy rain, rideshare applications like Uber and DiDi offer reliable door-to-door transit. Avoid hailing unmarked street taxis on the fly; if you must take a street cab, use an official sitio taxi stand or ask your accommodation to call one directly.

## Local Tip

Standard guidebooks frequently overlook the impact of the rainy season, which runs consistently from late May through October. Almost like clockwork, heavy downpours strike the city between 16:00 and 19:00 nearly every afternoon. When these torrential rains hit, street drainage slows down, surface traffic grinds to a complete standstill, and rideshare surge pricing skyrockets while driver availability drops. Plan your daily itinerary so that you are safely settled inside a museum, market, coffee shop, or restaurant by 16:00, saving your long transit trips across town for the dry morning hours.
