# Marrakech: Where to Go in 5 Days
![View of historic red walls and lush gardens under a blue sky in Marrakesh, Morocco.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/15360686/pexels-photo-15360686.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)
*Photo by [Moussa Idrissi](https://www.pexels.com/@mographe)*

Marrakech is a sensory surge of terra-cotta alleyways, ringing copper workshops, and heavy aromas of cumin and orange blossom. For first-time visitors, the Red City demands patience and a willingness to step away from modern comforts, balancing the frantic energy of its fortified 12th-century Medina with quiet interludes in cool riad courtyards and sleek modern districts.

Navigating this city well means looking past manicured photos to understand its distinct rhythm. By splitting your time between ancient trading alleys, the French colonial neighborhood of Guéliz, and the surrounding stone desert, you will experience the authentic pulse of a trade hub that has welcomed travelers for nearly a millennium.

## 5 Days in Marrakech

### Day 1
Neighborhood route: Koutoubia Mosque to Southern Medina and Jemaa el-Fnaa.
Timing: 8:30 AM to 7:00 PM.
Begin your orientation outside Koutoubia Mosque, strolling through its palm gardens before moving down Rue Royale into the Kasbah. Explore the Saadian Tombs and the red-clay ruins of El Badi Palace. In the late afternoon, head into Jemaa el-Fnaa as the main square fills with food stalls and storytellers.
Practical warning: Unsolicited local guides near the Saadian Tombs will tell you the palace is closed for prayer to reroute you toward leather shops; decline firmly and keep moving toward the official entrance.

### Day 2
Neighborhood route: Central Souks to Ben Youssef Madrasa.
Timing: 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM.
Trace a northern line through Souk Semmarine and Souk El Kebir. Visit the restored 14th-century Ben Youssef Madrasa to see its mosaic zellige tiles, then explore the nearby Marrakech Museum and Le Jardin Secret for afternoon tea away from the crowded alleys.
Practical warning: Motorbikes speed through tight souk corridors without warning; keep strictly to the right side of any path and never step backward without looking behind you first.

### Day 3
Neighborhood route: Guéliz and the Majorelle District.
Timing: 8:30 AM to 3:00 PM.
Leave the city walls for Guéliz, the neighborhood built during the French Protectorate. Start early at Jardin Majorelle and the Yves Saint Laurent Museum to admire cobalt architecture and desert flora. Afterward, walk down Avenue Mohammed V to browse contemporary art galleries and mid-century design boutiques.
Practical warning: Tickets for Jardin Majorelle are no longer sold at the door; you must buy them online at least 24 hours in advance or entry will be denied.

### Day 4
Neighborhood route: Mellah (Jewish Quarter) to Bahia Palace.
Timing: 9:30 AM to 5:00 PM.
Spend the morning inside Bahia Palace, inspecting painted cedar wood ceilings and tiled courtyards. Walk south into the historic Jewish Quarter (Mellah) to visit the Lazama Synagogue and Miaara Cemetery, finishing at Place des Ferblantiers where metalworkers shape tin lamps.
Practical warning: Herbalists in the Mellah often use intense sales pitches for traditional remedies; settle on prices before agreeing to sample or custom-mix any products.

### Day 5
Neighborhood route: Palmeraie and the Agafay Desert.
Timing: 2:00 PM to 9:30 PM.
Take a short trip outside the urban core. Spend the early afternoon driving through the historic palm groves of the Palmeraie before heading southwest to the Agafay stone desert. Watch the sunset over the arid hills and end your journey with a traditional dinner in a desert camp under the stars.
Practical warning: Avoid booking unaccredited quad-bike tours along the Palmeraie roadside; book through registered providers to ensure proper safety helmets and insurance coverage.

## Getting Around
The historic Medina is strictly pedestrian, meaning walking is your primary mode of transit inside the walls. Outside the ramparts, the simplest way to move is using the small yellow "Petit Taxis." These cars hold up to three passengers and run throughout the modern city. Always demand that the driver turns on the meter (*compteur*) as soon as you sit down. If the driver claims the meter is broken, refuse the ride and flag another cab down the street. For trips outside city bounds—such as the Agafay Desert or airport transfers—you must negotiate a flat rate with a "Grand Taxi" driver before departing, or catch the official Airport Bus Line 19, which costs 30 MAD per ride.

## Local Tip
Always carry a pocket full of small change (2, 5, and 10 MAD coins) because large bills like 100 or 200 MAD notes are nearly impossible to break with street vendors, taxi drivers, and small cafes. Beyond basic purchases, loose coins pay for the unwritten rules of Medina life: tipping public bathroom attendants, giving a few dirhams to teenagers who offer directions when your GPS cuts out in the covered alleys, or tipping luggage haulers pushing handcarts. Paying with a large note often forces vendors to walk several blocks to find change, slowing your day down and marking you immediately as an inexperienced tourist.
