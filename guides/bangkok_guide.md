# Bangkok: A 5-Day City Guide

Bangkok is a river city that visitors try to cross by taxi at the worst hour. This 5-day route uses the Chao Phraya for the palaces and temples, saves Chinatown for after dark, and gives one morning to a market that is not a mall.

## 5 Days in Bangkok

### Day 1: The Grand Palace early, then the river

Go to the Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew at opening, in clothes that cover shoulders and knees, with water and no expectation of a quick lap. The complex is large and the heat is the real limit. When you leave, walk to the river pier and ride a public boat downstream or across to Wat Arun if the light is still kind. A taxi back across town at 15:00 is the mistake. Stay on the river until you are ready to return to the hotel. Sukhumvit is a convenient base for the BTS and a poor base for this morning, so leave early.

### Day 2: A neighborhood of shophouses, then Chinatown at dusk

Use the hot middle of the day for a museum with air conditioning, the Museum of Siam or the National Museum, or simply for the hotel. The evening is Yaowarat. Eat as you walk: one noodle stall, one seafood stop if the price is posted, and a dessert you can carry. Chinatown is not a restaurant reservation district. It is a sequence of small decisions. Wat Traimit and the golden Buddha can be the late-afternoon anchor before the street fills. Do not add a rooftop bar on the same night unless the bar is already on the walk home.

### Day 3: One market, and only one

Chatuchak Weekend Market deserves a morning if you are in the city on a Saturday or Sunday, and it deserves an exit plan. Go at opening, decide on two sections, and leave before the heat and the crowds make every aisle look the same. On a weekday, Or Tor Kor is the better market: a real food hall next to Chatuchak's site, calmer and more useful for lunch. The afternoon can be a boat toward Khlong San or a simple mall if you need the cold air. A floating market outside the city replaces this day. It should not be added to it.

### Day 4: A last bowl, then the airport rail

The last morning is a bowl of jok or boat noodles near the hotel and a walk to the station. The Airport Rail Link is the straightforward ride to Suvarnabhumi. Don Mueang is the other airport and a different bus or rail problem. Leave at least three hours before an international departure.

### Day 5: Departure

Bangkok traffic does not care that the flight is soon.

## Getting Around Bangkok

The BTS and MRT cover most of the modern city. The Chao Phraya boats cover the old city better than a taxi. Buy a transit card or use the ticket machines, and do not plan a cross-town taxi between 16:00 and 19:00. A river hotel saves a day. A Sukhumvit hotel saves the nights. Pick one and accept the commute on the other days.

## Local Tip

If a street stall will not point at a price, keep walking. The famous rooms, including the ones with a long queue and a theatrical wok, are a single meal on the trip, not the template for every lunch. A neighborhood noodle shop at 11:30 is the meal that tells you whether you understand the city.
