# Buenos Aires: A 3-Day City Guide
![View of historic architecture and skyline in Buenos Aires, Argentina on a sunny day.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/14401405/pexels-photo-14401405.png?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)
*Photo by [Matheus De Moraes Gugelmim](https://www.pexels.com/@matheus-de-moraes-gugelmim-38060312)*

Buenos Aires rewards visitors who slow down and adapt to its unique rhythm. As the capital of Argentina, this sprawling metropolis balances grand nineteenth-century European architecture with an unmistakably South American energy. From the leafy avenues of Recoleta to the gritty, bohemian corners of San Telmo, the city’s identity lives in its distinct neighborhoods, street-level cafes, and vibrant culinary scene.

For first-time travelers, navigating Buenos Aires requires adjusting to local time. Lunch rarely starts before 1:00 PM, afternoon coffee is essential to bridge the gap, and dinner spots remain quiet until at least 9:30 PM. Expect high-energy night walks, rich red wines, world-class beef, and a passionate culture that moves at its own unhurried pace.

## 3 Days in Buenos Aires

### Day 1: Historic Core and San Telmo

Route: Start your morning at Plaza de Mayo to observe the Casa Rosada and the Metropolitan Cathedral. Walk south along Calle Defensa, entering the historic neighborhood of San Telmo. Spend your afternoon wandering between antique shops, colonial courtyards, and the covered Mercado de San Telmo. In the late afternoon, head west toward El Ateneo Grand Splendid in Recoleta, a majestic former theater converted into one of the world's most famous bookstores.
Timing: 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM for the historic plaza; 1:30 PM to 5:00 PM for San Telmo; 6:00 PM for El Ateneo.
Practical Warning: Keep your phone tucked away while standing near busy intersections along Avenida 9 de Julio, as drive-by phone snatches by passing motorcyclists are common in this high-traffic corridor.

### Day 2: Recoleta Culture and Palermo Parks

Route: Begin at Recoleta Cemetery to explore its elaborate marble mausoleums, then walk to the nearby Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes. After lunch, transit north to Palermo Soho and Palermo Hollywood. Spend the afternoon exploring independent boutiques, tree-lined residential streets, and the sprawling greenery of the Bosques de Palermo. End your evening at a neighborhood steakhouse.
Timing: 10:00 AM to 1:00 PM in Recoleta; 2:30 PM to 6:30 PM exploring Palermo; 9:30 PM onward for dinner.
Practical Warning: Recoleta Cemetery requires non-resident foreign tourists to pay an entrance fee strictly by credit or debit card at the gate; cash payments are not accepted at the ticket booth.

### Day 3: La Boca Waterfront and Puerto Madero

Route: Head south to La Boca to view the colorful tin houses along Caminito and check out contemporary art at Fundación PROA. Mid-day, take a short ride north to Puerto Madero, the city's modernized docklands. Walk across the Puente de la Mujer and enjoy an unhurried afternoon promenade along the waterfront or inside the Reserva Ecológica Costanera Sur. Conclude your trip with an evening tango performance or a visit to a local neighborhood milonga.
Timing: 9:30 AM to 12:30 PM in La Boca; 1:30 PM to 5:30 PM in Puerto Madero; 9:00 PM for dinner and tango.
Practical Warning: Stay strictly within the heavily policed four-block tourist perimeter of Caminito in La Boca; crossing into the surrounding residential side streets can put you at immediate risk of mugging.

## Getting Around
The most efficient way to navigate public transit is with a SUBE card, a rechargeable smart card valid on the Subte subway network and all city buses. You can purchase and top up a SUBE card at main subway stations and designated street kiosks showing the SUBE logo. The Subte is fast and simple to navigate, though lines shut down around 11:00 PM. For late-night travel or direct point-to-point routes, use ride-hailing applications like Cabify or Uber, which offer tracked routes and upfront pricing. If you prefer a street taxi, only hail black-and-yellow cars marked with an official Radio Taxi sign.

## Local Tip
Do not attempt to eat dinner at 7:30 PM, as traditional kitchens will be closed and casual dining rooms will feel completely empty. Instead, adopt the local habit of the afternoon *merienda* between 5:00 PM and 7:00 PM. Locals pause their afternoon to sit at neighborhood cafes for a *cortado* (espresso cut with a small splash of warm milk) paired with *facturas* (sweet pastries, particularly *medialunas*) or a toasted ham-and-cheese sandwich on thin white bread (*tostado*). Embracing this late snack bridges the long gap between lunch and dinner, letting you comfortably wait for a 9:30 PM table when restaurant atmospheres are at their best.
